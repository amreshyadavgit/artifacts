## 1. Summary
From 08:01 UTC every client of fhir-lite-api saw slow responses, 500/504 errors and, twice, 503s with no ready pods. The trigger was Clinic C-017's new ward dashboard, which from 08:00 asked `$lastn` for all 512 of its patients every 30 s from 24 workstations, one hour after 208,896 historical observations were imported for those patients. `ObservationService.lastN` runs two queries per subject and loads each subject's full history, so each dashboard request issued 1,024 statements and hydrated about 209,000 rows, which saturated CPU and heap, exhausted the connection pool and made both pods fail their probes. Liveness failures restarted the pods into the same load. Service recovered at 08:34 after the dashboard refresh was disabled at 08:31; the permanent fix is a set-based `$lastn` with a subject cap.

## 7. Root cause
Causal chain:
1. CHG-4471 gave 512 patients an average history of 408 observations, about 30 times the rest of the population (baseline 14 rows per call, E9 07:30 to 08:00).
2. CHG-4472 sent one `$lastn` request for all 512 patients every 30 s from 24 workstations: 0.8 req/s, observed 0.86 to 0.97 req/s before retries (E2, E6).
3. `ObservationService.lastN` (`sample-app/src/main/java/org/example/fhir/service/ObservationService.java:69-74`) issues `patients.findById` plus `findByPatientIdOrderByEffectiveDateTimeDesc` per subject and filters by code in Java: 1,024 statements and about 209,000 hydrated rows per request (E9, E10). All of them stay in the persistence context until the read-only transaction ends.
4. With a 1-CPU limit and about 576Mi of heap (`MaxRAMPercentage=75` of 768Mi), a handful of concurrent requests throttled the CPU and drove the JVM into long GC stalls (E3 housekeeper deltas 38 to 64 s, E6 throttling 0.8).
5. Each request held one of 10 pool connections for 36 to 60 s, so the pool emptied and every other endpoint waited 30 s for a connection and failed (E3, E4).
6. Probe requests could not be answered within 1 s: readiness failed first, concentrating traffic on the other pod, then liveness killed both (E5, E7). Restarted pods received the same retried load and failed again; scaling out added pods and DB load but not headroom per request (E6 08:19 to 08:25).

- **Trigger**: CHG-4472 (dashboard go-live), on top of CHG-4471 (history import).
- **Root cause**: the per-subject N+1 in `ObservationService.lastN` (marked `TEACHING-DEFECT(perf-n+1)`) combined with no upper bound on `subjects` (`sample-app/src/main/java/org/example/fhir/api/ObservationController.java:43`). Cost grows with subjects x history depth, and nothing rejected a 512-subject request.
- **Contributing factors**: liveness and readiness probes on the traffic connector with the 1 s default timeout; HikariCP 30 s connection timeout and no statement timeout; no application metrics (`include: health,info`), so the dashboard was identified only at about 08:26 from ingress user agents and AUDIT `results=512`; onboarding load test with 20 patients x 2 observations; the vendor client shares the `clinician` account, so it could not be rate-limited or throttled separately.
