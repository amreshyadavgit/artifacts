## 1. Summary
From 08:00 UTC, 20 ward tablets at Clinic KE-C-017 each called `lastn` with 100 subjects every 30 seconds. On spice_lite 0.4.1, `lastn` issues two queries per subject and reads every matching observation of each patient, and this clinic's patients had just been loaded with about 423 heart-rate readings each. Each call held one of the 9 gunicorn sync workers for 14 s to over 120 s, so by 08:02 every worker was busy and every endpoint on the site, including desk and login, returned 504 or 502 until the clinic switched the tablets' refresh off at 08:31. The permanent fix is the set-based `lastn` (3 queries for any number of subjects), plus a per-client rate limit and a cost-based cap.

## 7. Root cause
- **Trigger:** CHG-5103 put 20 tablets live that each call `lastn` with 100 subjects every 30 s, about 40 calls per minute (E1, E11).
- **Root cause:** `spice_lite.api.fhir.lastn` costs two queries per subject and reads every matching observation of each subject to keep one row (`TEACHING-DEFECT(perf-n+1)`, `sample-app/spice_lite/spice_lite/api/fhir.py:155-184`). A 100-subject call is 200 statements (E15) and, with this clinic's histories, about 42,300 rows hydrated as `frappe._dict` in one gunicorn worker (E5). `MAX_LASTN_SUBJECTS = 100` bounds the subjects, not that cost.

Causal chain:
1. 40 calls per minute at 100 subjects each: 4,000 subjects and 8,000 statements per minute (E1, E11); pg_stat_statements shows about 3,800 per-subject observation queries per minute, fewer because timed-out calls stopped early (E5).
2. Each statement reads about 423 rows with `select *`, so each call hydrates about 42,300 rows (E5).
3. Each call holds one sync gunicorn worker for 14 s uncontended (E3); 40 calls per minute need about 9 worker-minutes per minute before contention, so the 9 workers are saturated by 08:02 (E11).
4. With every worker busy, all other requests wait in the listen queue: desk, login, search and writes time out at nginx's 120 s (504) or fail when a stuck worker is killed (502) (E6, E7).
5. The same load keeps `ke-db-1` busy (71 to 88% CPU), so the 5-minute reminder job exceeds the 300 s `default` queue timeout and the queue backs up (E9, E10).

**Contributing factors:**
- CHG-5102 had just given each KE-C-017 patient about 423 heart-rate observations; the staging load test used 5 patients with 2 observations each (E1).
- One integration token shared by all tablets and no per-client rate limit, so one client could consume the whole site (E1).
- gunicorn sync workers serve the desk, integrations and login from one pool of 9, so a slow endpoint takes the whole site down (brief).
- No alert on busy workers or on per-endpoint latency; detection came from the 5xx ratio, 9 minutes after the first slow calls (timeline).
- On Postgres, `SL Encounter.patient` has no index despite `search_index: 1` (KNOWN_DEFECTS.md D-6), which is why the desk patient form's encounter query took 1.3 s under load (postgres-slow.log:8).
- The restart at 08:12 was the right first move but could only buy minutes, because the load came back with the next tablet refresh (E12).
